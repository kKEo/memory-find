# Eval report — v0.7.0 (P3: the lab)

*Measured 2026-10-02 with `memo-mcp eval` on an Apple Silicon laptop, hugot v0.7.2 pure-Go
backend. Two corpora: the notes corpus (79 documents, 29 queries; 2 abstention queries) and the
knowledge-base corpus (314 documents, 19 queries; 2 abstention, 2 paraphrase queries that only
run with a real model). Document granularity, top 10. Quality columns are means over the queries
that have a right answer; abstention is the share of no-match queries that returned nothing;
p50 is query latency including query embedding; tokens p50 is the size of the returned
passages. The `hash` rows are the deterministic test embedder the CI gate uses.*

Reproduce: `memo-mcp eval --models hash,minilm,potion,granite-small-r2 --profiles
default,keyword-only,semantic-only,minmax,precise --rerank --agent-proxy --format table`.
Loading the corpora took 1.7 s (hash), 179 s (minilm), 0.5 s (potion) and 440 s (granite).

## What the numbers say

1. **The embedder matters most where words fail.** On the lexical notes corpus every embedder
   scores the same nDCG (0.966–0.978): keywords carry it. On the knowledge-base corpus, whose two
   paraphrase queries share no words with their answers, MiniLM and potion score 0 on both,
   granite finds both (MRR 0.33 and 0.20) and lifts recall@5 from 0.882 to 1.000. That is the
   MemDelta result in miniature: the model is the lever for the questions a keyword index cannot
   answer.
2. **The hybrid beats either arm alone, with every real model.** semantic-only drops 2–20 points;
   keyword-only never finds a paraphrase. The two arms fail on different queries, which is the
   whole argument for fusion.
3. **Score fusion (minmax) edges out rank fusion (RRF) on the knowledge-base corpus** (+0.03 to
   +0.04 nDCG with MiniLM and granite) and is a wash on the notes corpus (−0.003 to −0.008).
   Consistent with OpenSearch's BEIR finding, not yet decisive here: RRF stays the default, minmax
   is one flag away, and the P4 slices will add evidence.
4. **Similarity scales are model-specific.** Granite's unrelated-text cosine (~0.6) is above
   MiniLM's "strong" band. Without per-model bands it never abstained; with them, it abstains on
   both no-match queries at no cost to quality. Bands now live on the model entry.
5. **The agent-iterating proxy adds nothing** on this corpus: the first round already finds what
   the second would. It remains the baseline derived knowledge (P7, P8) must beat.
6. **potion is the real surprise:** a lookup table with no network pass equals MiniLM on both
   corpora except paraphrase, at 6 ms per query and 0.5 s to load an entire corpus. It is the
   right instant tier and a strong fallback.
7. **Cost.** Query latency: potion 6 ms, MiniLM 110–130 ms, granite 180–205 ms (1.6× MiniLM).
   Ingestion: granite embeds 2.5× slower than MiniLM (440 s vs 179 s for both corpora, about 700
   passages). Both ONNX models are an order of magnitude slower on this backend than they would be
   on ONNX Runtime; see `docs/spikes/S4-embedding-models.md`.

## The default-model decision (OD-6)

The OD-6 rule, recalibrated after S4 (no ONNX model meets 150 ms here; the incumbent takes
593 ms per passage at write time): *Apache/MIT licence, loads under GoMLX, beats MiniLM on nDCG@10
by at least 0.02, query p50 no worse than 2× MiniLM.* granite-small-r2 passes all four (+0.05 on
the knowledge-base corpus, equal on notes, 1.6× latency, Apache-2.0). It costs a 195 MB download
instead of 87 MB and 2.5× slower ingestion. The owner's decision is recorded in `docs/roadmap.md`
§9.3 under OD-6.

## How to read a miss

`memo-mcp eval --models minilm --profiles default --format md --explain-failures` prints the
per-query table with the arms behind each first hit and one line per miss; for one query against a
real knowledge base, `memo-mcp explain "<query>" <memo://uri>` prints the full `Why` block.
